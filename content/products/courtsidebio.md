Link in bio took off as a product with creators. Once it did, the category split into niches. There are now link-in-bio tools built just for salons, for wellness professionals and for fitness influencers, each shaped around what that audience needs to show.

Racket sports coaching didn't have one. courtsidebio is the link in bio built for tennis, pickleball and padel coaches.

## Why independent coaches

Most sports software is built for clubs. It handles scheduling, memberships and court bookings, and it's priced for an organisation with a front desk. That works for a club. It doesn't help the coach who teaches at three different courts, finds students through Instagram and WhatsApp, and needs to fill their week without paying for software built for someone else.

What that coach needs is simpler: one page that answers every question a player asks before booking, and a way to grow without a big monthly bill.

## What goes on the page

Every section answers something players actually ask.

- **Level.** UTR, DUPR, NTRP or Playtomic ratings sit at the top, next to where the coach played and how long they've coached. Each rating is marked as self-reported, with a link to the source.
- **Lessons and prices.** Private coaching, hitting sessions and group clinics, each with a price and a length.
- **What players say.** A few words from current students.
- **Court times.** The coach sets weekly windows once. Players see the next seven days in the coach's time zone and tap a time to ask for it.

Tapping a time opens WhatsApp or email with the message already written: "I found your page and would like a hitting session on Tuesday at 6:00 PM. Are you free?" Coaches pick a grass, clay, hard court or night theme, so the page looks like their sport. Setup takes about ten minutes.

## What I left out

There's no booking calendar, no marketplace and no booking fee. Coaches already run their schedules their own way, and I didn't want to get in the middle of that. Showing typical court times is enough for a player to ask a specific question, and the coach confirms over the channel they already use. Nobody sits in between, and nobody takes a cut.

## Building a SaaS, not just a product

courtsidebio is also my first real attempt at a SaaS. Unlike my other projects, it has to work for people I'll never talk to. A coach has to find it, set up a page without help and keep it current, and the page has to bring them enough students to be worth it.

It's free to start, and the first test is simple: will coaches put the link in their bio and leave it there?
