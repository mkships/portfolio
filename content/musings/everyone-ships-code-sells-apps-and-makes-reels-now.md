I think every job is turning into a job where you ship code, sell apps and make reels. The titles haven’t caught up, but the work has.

## One week at Start.dev

My freelance role at Start.dev is the clearest example I have. It’s an interactive coding education platform, and in a normal week I do three jobs there.

- **Content.** I produce marketing videos end to end with agents and write blog posts.
- **Marketing.** I ran a Reddit-led push to get Start.dev mentioned in AI-generated answers, and its citations doubled.
- **Product.** I automate the Discord workflows and ship small product improvements myself.

Two years ago that was a video editor, a content marketer, a community manager and a developer. Now it’s one person with agents. I’m not exceptional at any of the four. The agents carry enough of the work that I can do all of them to a standard that ships.

## It isn’t only me

The companies that build and use these tools are saying the same thing.

- Microsoft’s 2025 Work Trend Index has a section titled “[Every employee becomes an agent boss](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/04/23/the-2025-annual-work-trend-index-the-frontier-firm-is-born/)”, meaning someone who builds, delegates to and manages agents.
- LinkedIn replaced its associate product manager programme with one for “full stack builders”, who are expected to take a product from idea to launch themselves. Its chief product officer [explained why](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-linkedin-is-replacing-pms) on Lenny’s Podcast.
- Shopify’s CEO told staff that using AI is now a baseline expectation, and that teams should show why AI can’t do the work [before asking for more people](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/ai-use-is-no-longer-optional-at-shopify-declares-ceo-tobi-lutke-in-internal-memo-471211-2025-04-08).
- PwC calls the pattern [role convergence](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/consulting/human-resources/role-convergence-ai-workforce-redesign.html), with jobs getting broader and measured by outcomes.

Not everyone agrees. An analysis of job postings by The New Stack found that [specialists are still what employers ask for](https://thenewstack.io/ai-engineering-roles-emerging/). I think both are true, and the next section is why.

## What still has to be yours

We used to talk about T-shaped people, with one deep skill and a broad top. I think AI makes it Pi-shaped: one deep mastery, and real ability in two more tracks.

The depth matters more than before. I’m not an engineer, but once I had programming fundamentals, working with a coding agent changed. I could debate the data model with it and steer it, and the output stopped feeling like slop. Without that, I’d only be able to say “I don’t like this”.

The same goes for deciding what’s worth doing. An agent will make the video, the post or the feature. It won’t tell you which one the business needs this week.
