Wimbledon is one of the oldest sporting events in modern history. It is patronised by the "Royal Family" themselves and it is a heritage for the Britishers.

Comparing that with "China Open" - something that started only decades back itself is a bit of stretch.

Tennis is one of the few sports where you go "pro" - a term that is not synonymous with any sport in India. Most people can't comprehend this at all.

-> If you want to be a cricketer - you'd aspire to play for India/your country. (meaning it is a federation sport)

-> If you want to be a badminton player - you'd aspire to represent India at Olympics/Asian Games etc. (same, it is a federation sport)

-> If you want to be a tennis pro - you'd aspire to win a grand-slam like Wimbledon which you'd "keep with you". You don't even need to wear a Indian jersey while playing or even while receiving the tropy.

People say "Novak Djokovic won Wimbledon" and not Serbia won 24 slams for the very reason.

Risk in tennis is the highest, you bet on yourself, you train for yourself and you play for yourself. Hence the reward ratio is also the highest.

And in tennis you tour from North America to South America to Europe to Asia to Middle East to North Africa while 90% of badminton is concentrated in just East Asia. So no, even the travel expenses are not the same.
