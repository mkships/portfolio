When I can build a working prototype in an afternoon, I can spend more of the week figuring out where it belongs in someone’s day. That’s the part of product work I want to give more time to.

I used to spend a lot of time describing how something might work. With coding agents, I can get to a version I can try for myself much sooner. A working flow makes some decisions wonderfully cheap. A few minutes with a new filter can tell me more about whether it belongs on the page than a long discussion about where to put it.

## Spend more time on the handoff

[Court Trips](/products/court-trips) started with a problem I’d run into myself. I wanted to find another tennis trip, and the options were scattered across club sites, resort pages and player recommendations. A directory helps with that part. Once someone finds a trip, they can confirm dates, availability and booking terms with the organiser.

I could prototype a checkout for the directory, too. The bigger decision is what a player would expect from us after paying. Who confirms the spot? Who handles a cancellation? Those questions deserve time because the answers shape the service a player gets long after they’ve clicked a button.

## Give each prototype a question to answer

For the next version, I’d rather learn whether the directory helps someone narrow a messy search to a few trips they’d actually consider. I can put a working version in front of someone planning a trip and watch where they pause, what they compare and when they’re ready to visit the organiser’s site. That gives me a better question for the next afternoon of building.
