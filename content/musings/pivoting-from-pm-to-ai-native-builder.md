I think the traditional product management role is shrinking. AI can help with long requirements documents, drafting tickets and summarising conversations between design and engineering. That changes how I want to spend my time.

## Roles are being redrawn around the work

Boris Cherny, who leads Claude Code at Anthropic, [described five kinds of people](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2071379474277613732) he sees on his team. None of them is a job title.

- **Prototypers** come up with new ideas and turn out many of them, most of which never ship.
- **Builders** take a prototype and make it a real product.
- **Sweepers** clean up, simplify, and remove what isn’t needed.
- **Growers** take something that’s built and keep improving it until more people want it.
- **Maintainers** keep a mature system secure, reliable and fast.

His point is that these don’t map to functions. A designer, an engineer or a PM can be any of them, and most people cover two. I found it a useful way to look at my own move.

## Where the old PM job sat

For most of my career I wrote specs for engineering to implement. That work sits between the five. I described what the prototype should be, and engineering built it, cleaned it up and kept it running.

The part of that job that was mine alone was the documents, the tickets and the cross-functional work, which often meant a full day of meetings. AI now helps most with the documents and the tickets. The meetings are still the job.

Every product team has also had the PM whose whole job was moving tickets from one column to the next. No building, no sweeping, not even growing. A Jira board with a salary. I don’t think that role survives what’s coming.

## Prototyper and builder

I went from writing specs to writing prompts I could turn into working software. With Claude Code, Replit and Cursor, I started shipping prototypes and full apps.

My workflow is to define the problem, give the agent specific instructions and try the result myself. When I can get to working software quickly, I can spend more time asking whether it solves a problem worth solving.

That’s how I built this portfolio, the projects in Tinkertank and the interactive widgets on the site. Building alone, I have to be the prototyper, the builder and the sweeper myself.

## What stays with me

I still have to decide what to build next and what to turn down. That means weighing what people need, what makes sense for the business and what we can realistically ship.

I rebuilt my workflow around AI tools so I could spend more time making those calls and trying ideas myself.

## The bet I’m making

I want to spend more time building. I’m curious how far one person can go with a clear idea of what users need and the ability to build a working prototype in hours.

I think the PMs who thrive will be the ones who can see how the parts of a product fit together, test an idea themselves and make a clear call about what to ship. That’s the bet I’m making.
